THE CHALLENGE

An EOT is not established by proving that a delay event occurred. The analysis must show when, how and to what extent it affected contractual completion.

Major projects develop through multiple programme revisions, progress updates, instructions, access constraints, design changes, approvals, variations and other events. The critical path may move over time, while logic changes, calendars, constraints, float consumption, resequencing and mitigation can materially affect the delay position.

A defensible EOT analysis therefore needs to connect the contractual event to the relevant programme activities, establish the timing and period of impact, examine criticality and concurrent delay, and explain the resulting effect on a contractual completion date or milestone. Delay & EOT Analysis is designed to organise that process around traceable programme data and contemporaneous project records.

KEY CAPABILITIES

From programme records to a structured delay position.

01

Programme Data & Baseline Review

Organise baseline, accepted or approved programmes, periodic updates, revised programmes, lookaheads and progress information. Review available activity dates, logic, calendars, constraints, milestones, float and progress data before detailed delay analysis begins. Programme information from Oracle Primavera P6, Microsoft Project and comparable scheduling environments can be incorporated subject to the available data and professional validation.

02

Delay Event & Chronology Mapping

Identify and classify potential delay events and reconstruct when they arose, how long they persisted and which records support them. Instructions, access issues, approvals, design information, variations, interfaces, authority matters and other project events can be mapped into a coordinated chronology.

03

Contractual Entitlement & Notice Mapping

Connect each delay event with the relevant contractual provisions, notice requirements, procedural steps and potential EOT entitlement. The workflow can support FIDIC, NEC, amended standard forms and bespoke contracts, while keeping contractual analysis separate from programme impact assessment.

04

Critical & Driving Path Review

Map events to affected activities and review whether those activities were critical, near-critical or driving the relevant milestone during the period under consideration. Track how the critical or driving path may have changed across successive programme updates rather than relying on a single static programme.

05

Delay Methodology Support

Structure programme and factual inputs for professional analysis using recognised approaches such as Time Impact Analysis, windows or time-slice analysis, As-Planned versus As-Built, Impacted As-Planned or other appropriate methodologies. Method selection and final analytical conclusions remain subject to the project records and professional delay-planning judgment.

06

Concurrency, Float & Mitigation

Identify periods requiring review for potential concurrent delay, float availability or consumption, mitigation, acceleration, resequencing and recovery measures. Bring contractor, employer and neutral project events into a common timeline so competing causes can be examined transparently.

07

EOT Period Assessment

Structure the link between event duration, programme impact period, criticality and the resulting movement of contractual completion or interim milestones. Separate event duration from compensable or excusable time impact so the requested extension is supported by a clear analytical path.

08

Analysis Narrative & Submission Assembly

Convert validated programme findings, contract entitlement and contemporaneous records into a coordinated EOT analysis. Organise methodology, event-by-event reasoning, programme references, charts, appendices and evidentiary links for interim submissions, detailed claims, assessments or dispute preparation.

TYPICAL OUTPUTS

Outputs built for planners, contract teams and decision-makers.

The objective is not simply to produce a delay narrative, but to create a traceable working record showing how programme evidence, contractual entitlement and factual events support the EOT position.

01

Programme Data Readiness Review

A structured review of available baseline and update programmes, programme versions, progress records and data gaps requiring clarification before analysis.

02

Delay Event Register

Potential delay events organised by date, cause, responsibility, contractual basis, affected period, relevant records and current analysis status.

03

Baseline & Update Comparison Schedule

Key changes between programme versions identified for review, including logic, dates, progress, constraints, milestones and changes affecting the critical or driving path.

04

Programme-to-Event Mapping

Delay events linked to programme activities, milestones and analysis periods so factual events can be traced directly to the relevant programme context.

05

Critical / Driving Path Review

A structured review of the activities and paths influencing contractual completion or relevant milestones during the periods affected by the claimed events.

06

Concurrency, Float & Mitigation Matrix

Potential concurrent events, float issues, mitigation measures, resequencing and recovery actions organised by period for professional assessment.

07

EOT Entitlement & Period Matrix

Each event mapped to contractual entitlement, notices, impact period, affected completion or milestone and the extension potentially supported by the analysis.

08

EOT Analysis & Evidence Pack

A coordinated report framework combining methodology, event chronologies, programme references, records, entitlement and the proposed EOT position for professional review.
